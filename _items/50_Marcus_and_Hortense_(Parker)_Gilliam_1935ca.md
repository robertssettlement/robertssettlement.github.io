Photograph of Marcus Gilliam and Hortense (Parker) Gilliam circa 1935.

Marcus Gilliam was born in Sheridan, Indiana to Sarah Ann Winburn and Elijah Gilliam, August 23, 1867.

He attended Sheridan Common Schools. At the age of 16 (according to his Noblesville Ledger obituary), he taught at Schoolhouse No. 5, and Roberts Settlement. He later he taught at the Noblesville Colored School and the colored school in Rockville, Indiana.

Marcus attended Northern Indiana Normal School (later named Valparaiso University) in 1886-1887 where he completed teacher preparatory classes.

He acquired a Master’s from Cornell University circa 1901.

Marcus married Clara Loucks (born 1867 in New York) in 1900 in Spencer, New York while studying at Cornell University. According to a newspaper account, he was the first black man to compete in the “Woodford Prize Contest in Oratory” at Cornell in his senior year.

He was appointed Principal at McCoy School (Indianapolis, Indiana) in 1902 and in 1905 served as principal of the Douglass School. In August 1905 assumed the role of Principal at a school in Buxton, Iowa.

In 1908 Marcus and Clara moved to St. Louis, Missouri where he was a teacher in several segregated schools (Garnett, Delaney, Sumner H.S).

Marcus divorced Clara in 1912 and married Hortense Parker in 1913.

He was appointed principal of the Waring School in St. Louis circa 1920 and remained in that capacity until retirement.

Marcus died in St. Louis, Missouri December 23, 1945. He is buried at Douglas Cemetery, E. St. Louis, Missouri.

Hortense Parker was born circa 1865 in Ripley, Ohio to Miranda (Boulden) and John P. Parker.

Her father John Parker bought his freedom from enslavement and became a well known abolitionist and conductor on the Underground railroad. For more information see: https://www.nps.gov/nr/travel/underground/oh2.htm

Hortense was the first known and recorded black graduate of Mount Holyoke College in Massachusetts (class of 1883) where she studied music.

She taught schools in the Midwest.

Hortense married Marcus James Gilliam in 1913.

She died December 9, 1938 in St. Louis, Missouri.
Initially she was buried at  Douglas Cemetery where later Marcus was buried. Their graves were later relocated in the 1960s for construction of I-64, their location is presently undetermined, but they are likely at either Booker T. Washington or Sunset Garden of Memory Cemeteries according to information attached to their profiles on www.findagrave.com.
